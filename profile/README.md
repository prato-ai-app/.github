# Prato AI

**AI that understands food.**

You take a photo of your dinner. Prato knows what it is, what's probably in it, how it was likely made, roughly what it's doing to your body, and what you could cook next with the same stuff.

That's the whole idea.

## The Problem With Food Apps Today

Food images? One app. Recipes? Another. Nutrition? A third. Meal planning? A fourth. Personal preferences? Nowhere.

Every food app treats these as separate problems. They're not. They're the same problem wearing different hats.

A meal *is* its ingredients, its method, its nutrition, its context. Pull them apart and you lose the thread.

Prato AI is built to keep them together.

## What a Picture of a Meal Actually Contains

More than the name of a dish. A lot more.

A useful food system should be able to look at a plate and ask:

- What is this?
- What's likely inside it?
- How was it probably made?
- What would the recipe look like?
- Roughly what's the nutrition?
- How could I change it?
- What else could I cook with similar ingredients?

Those aren't seven features. They're one question asked seven ways.

## Food Understanding

We're teaching machines to actually *see* food — not just slap a label on it.

A future Prato system looks at a meal, breaks it into components, infers ingredients and prep methods, and connects the whole thing to recipes and nutrition.

The pipeline:

Food Image → Understanding → Ingredients → Recipe → Nutrition → Personalization

## Reverse Recipe Generation

This is the fun one.

Normal cooking: you start with a recipe, you get a dish.

Prato: you start with the dish — a photo — and work backwards. Ingredients, quantities, technique, cuisine. Reconstruct the recipe from the plate.

It's a bridge between computer vision and cooking. Point your camera at lunch, get a recipe back.

## Nutrition That Actually Means Something

Nutrition numbers are useless in a vacuum. "320 calories" means nothing without the actual meal in front of you.

Prato connects nutrition to real food — ingredients, portions, alternatives — and eventually to *you*: your history, your preferences, what you actually eat.

Not a database of numbers. A picture of your plate, with meaning attached.

## Recipes That Behave

Recipes shouldn't be dead web pages you scroll past six ads on.

A recipe should be something a system can *understand* and *transform* — generate it, scale it, swap ingredients, adapt it to what's in your fridge, or build something new from what you already love.

## Food AI Agents

Down the line, Prato grows into agents that know your food.

Your meals, your recipes, your history. Ask it things. Build new dishes from old ones. Get recommendations that actually fit you — because they're based on you, not a generic algorithm.

## Where This Goes

A food intelligence layer. One system that connects:

Images → Ingredients → Recipes → Nutrition → Preferences → Recommendations

Not ten disconnected features. One thing that works together.
